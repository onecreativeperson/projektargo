Every site with PFAS foam now needs its own plan.

Since 23 October 2026, users of firefighting foam with 1 mg/L or more of PFAS must keep a written management plan for each place of use, review it every year, keep it for at least 15 years and label foam stock and foam waste. The dates on the hose line

Entry 82 bans PFAS in firefighting foams step by step. The conditions of use, including the management plan, already apply to every user who keeps using PFAS foam during a transition period.

23 Oct 2026

Management plan, labelling of stock and waste, separate collection and treatment for every user of PFAS foam.

23 Apr 2027

End of PFAS foam for training and testing (functional tests of fire systems stay allowed if all releases are contained) and for public fire services, with an exception for industrial fires at Seveso establishments.

23 Oct 2030

General ban on placing on the market and use of PFAS foams at 1 mg/L or more.

31 Dec 2030

End of use in portable fire extinguishers.

23 Oct 2035

End of the derogation for Seveso establishments (civilian airports excluded), offshore oil and gas installations, military vessels and ships with foam on board before 23 Oct 2025.

Source: Commission Regulation (EU) 2025/1988 of 2 October 2025, Annex, entry 82, points 1, 5, 6, 7 and 9 (eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2025/1988/oj).

1 mg/Llimit for the sum of all PFAS in the foam. Foam from equipment cleaned with best available techniques may have up to 50 mg/L.
15years: the minimum time the plan must stay available for inspection by the competent authorities. It is reviewed every year.
59%of the annual EU tonnage of PFAS-containing firefighting foam is used by the oil and (petro)chemical industry.

Source: Regulation (EU) 2025/1988, entry 82, points 1, 4 and 7(d); recital 7.

What the plan must contain

Point 7(d) lists five parts. The plan is specific to the place where the PFAS foam is used, so one company with three sites needs three plans.

  1. i

    Use and volumes. Details of the use conditions and volumes of foam on site, documenting how emissions and human exposure are kept as low as technically and practically possible.

  2. ii

    Collection and treatment. How stock of unused foam and PFAS-containing waste, including wastewater, is collected separately and treated so that the PFAS content is destroyed or irreversibly transformed.

  3. iii

    Cleaning and maintenance. The type and methods of cleaning and maintenance of the equipment.

  4. iv

    Leaks and spills. The plans for an accidental leakage or spillage of foam and, where relevant, documentation of the follow-up actions.

  5. v

    Switch to fluorine-free foam. A strategy for substituting PFAS-containing foams with fluorine-free foams.

Class B only

PFAS foam may only be used for fires involving flammable liquids.

Label stock and waste

Visible, legible and indelible, in the official language of the Member State, with the wording set in point 10.

Treatment

The recitals exclude biological wastewater treatment and incineration below 1 100 °C as adequate treatment.

Source: Regulation (EU) 2025/1988, entry 82, points 7 to 10; recitals 12, 39 and 40. Portable fire extinguishers are excluded from the plan obligation (recital 12).

FAQ

Do portable fire extinguishers need a plan?

No. The plan obligation applies to users of firefighting foam except in portable fire extinguishers (recital 12). Portable extinguishers have their own dates: placing on the market until 23 Oct 2026 (alcohol-resistant foam until 23 Apr 2027) and use until 31 Dec 2030.

We are a Seveso establishment. Do we have until 2035?

Use of PFAS foam is allowed until 23 Oct 2035, but since 23 Oct 2026 only under the conditions of point 7: class B fires only, minimised emissions, separate collection, labelling and the management plan. Civilian airports are not covered by this derogation.

We do not know whether our foam contains PFAS.

Choose “Not sure” in the form. The Snapshot lists what to ask your supplier for (product name, safety data sheet, fluorine content). The regulation says total fluorine methods may be used to show compliance (recital 27).

We service fire systems for clients. Can we use it?

Yes. Describe one client site; the Snapshot shows what the client needs and you can offer the plan as part of your service.

Is this legal advice?

No. The Snapshot is an indicative check based on your answers and the published regulation. It does not replace the competent authority, your fire safety specialist or a lawyer.
