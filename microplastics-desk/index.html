REACH Annex XVII · entry 78 · synthetic polymer microparticles

Your first microplastics report to ECHA is due 31 May 2027.

If you make or import paints, coatings, printing inks, adhesives, sealants or polishes, some of your polymers may count as synthetic polymer microparticles. Send up to 5 products and get a free SPM Readiness Snapshot by e-mail within 2 working days.

The definition in one line

≤ 5 mm
all dimensions of the particle (fibres ≤ 15 mm, length-to-diameter ratio above 3)
solid
the polymer is solid and makes up at least 1 % by weight of the particle
≥ 1 %
of the particles by weight meet the size criterion
outside
natural polymers not chemically modified, degradable polymers, solubility above 2 g/L, no carbon atoms

Source: Commission Regulation (EU) 2023/2055, Annex XVII entry 78, point 1 and exclusions (EUR-Lex).

Why now

Entry 78 runs on fixed dates. Two of them are already behind us.

  1. 17 Oct 2025

    Instructions and statement

    Suppliers relying on a paragraph 5 derogation (the polymer stays contained, forms a film, or ends up in a solid matrix) must pass on instructions for use and disposal. FEICA advises adding the entry 78 statement, for example in SDS section 15.

    Regulation (EU) 2023/2055, entry 78 points 7–8; FEICA guidance on the SPM restriction

  2. 31 May 2026

    First wave reported

    Industrial sites using pellets, flakes and powders as feedstock for plastics filed the first reports, covering 2025. IUCLID and REACH-IT have new releases for this reporting.

    Entry 78 point 11; EU Transition Pathways, 3 Dec 2025, on ECHA’s release of 26 Nov 2025

  3. 31 May 2027

    Your first report

    Suppliers that place products on the market for the first time under derogations 4(b), 4(d), 4(e) or paragraph 5 report end uses, generic polymer identity and estimated releases for 2026. Other manufacturers and downstream users at industrial sites report from 2027 too.

    Entry 78 points 11–12; FEICA: first report for year 2026 due 31 May 2027

  4. Every 31 May

    Then every year

    The report repeats annually. FEICA notes that no minimum quantity is set for reporting or for supplier information.

    FEICA guidance on the SPM restriction

CEPE, the European paint industry association, says the polymeric content of binders and certain additives in paints may fall under the SPM definition: the use is derogated from the ban, but information and reporting obligations remain.

cepe.org/microplastics

Questions

Are polymer dispersions covered?

Entry 78 covers solid polymers. FEICA notes that a large number of polymer dispersions are outside the scope because they are liquid. The Snapshot flags which products need a confirmation from your supplier.

Is there a minimum quantity?

The FEICA guidance sets no minimum quantity for reporting or for supplier information.

Who files the report?

For products: the supplier that places them on the market for the first time under derogation 4(b), 4(d), 4(e) or paragraph 5 (entry 78 point 12). Industrial-site users report under point 11.
