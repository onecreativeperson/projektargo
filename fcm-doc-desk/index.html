Why now

The DoC rules changed. The transition is over.

Regulation (EU) 2025/351 amended Regulation (EU) No 10/2011 on plastic materials and articles intended to come into contact with food. Among other changes, it replaced point 6 of Annex IV and added points 10 and 11 – the content every declaration of compliance must have.

21 Feb 2025

Commission adopts Regulation (EU) 2025/351. Published in the Official Journal on 24 February 2025.

16 Dec 2025

Intermediates first placed on the market after this date that do not meet the new rules must say so in their DoC – and may only be used for articles placed on the market before 16 September 2026.

16 Sep 2026

Plastic materials that only meet the old rules may stay on the market until stocks run out only if they were first placed on the market before this date.

What we check

Annex IV, point by point

A written declaration is required at every marketing stage except retail (Article 15 of Regulation (EU) No 10/2011). It must contain the information below. The Gap Check marks each point as present, partial or missing.

  1. 1Identity and address of the business operator issuing the declaration
  2. 2Identity and address of the manufacturer or importer
  3. 3Identity of the material, article, intermediate or substance
  4. 4Date of the declaration
  5. 5Confirmation of compliance with Regulation 10/2011 and Articles 3, 11(5), 15 and 17 of Regulation (EC) No 1935/2004
  6. 6Information on restricted substances, including non-intentionally added substances (NIAS); for intermediates, identity and amount of listed substances changed
  7. 7Information on substances with a restriction in food: specific migration from tests or theoretical calculation
  8. 8Specifications on use: types of food, time and temperature, highest surface-to-volume ratio verified
  9. 9Where a functional barrier is used, confirmation of compliance with the barrier rules
  10. 10For batches intended for reprocessing: confirmation and, where appropriate, composition and reprocessing instructions new
  11. 11Where substances were manufactured from waste: confirmation of high purity under Article 8(1) new

Source: Regulation (EU) No 10/2011, Annex IV, consolidated text of 16 March 2025 – EUR-Lex

What you get

A short report you can forward to your supplier

The table shows each Annex IV point. Where the DoC gives a substance with a specific migration limit and its concentration, we calculate migration in the worst case: everything migrates into the food. If that stays under the limit, compliance can be supported by calculation (Article 16 allows calculations in supporting documents). If not, or if data are missing, the report says which test or data to ask for.

The report ends with a ready-to-send request for the missing information.

FAQ

Questions

Is this a legal opinion or a lab test?

No. The Gap Check is an indicative review of the document against the regulation text. It does not replace a laboratory migration test or legal advice. Responsibility for a DoC stays with the company that issues it.

Who has to issue a declaration of compliance?

Under Article 15 of Regulation (EU) No 10/2011 a written declaration must be available at the marketing stages other than retail – for plastic materials and articles, for intermediates and for substances used to make them. The European Commission's guidance on supply-chain information names manufacturers, importers and distributors among those who issue it.

Which materials are covered?

Plastic materials and articles under Regulation (EU) No 10/2011, including plastic multi-layers. Inks, adhesives, coatings, rubber and silicones are outside the free check.

Do I have to upload the document?

No. You can paste the text or describe what the DoC states. The upload is optional and used only to prepare your Gap Check.

Is there any cost or follow-up obligation?

No payment, no obligation. You can tell us in the form whether the paid version interests you.
