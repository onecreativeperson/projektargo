# Solve the "dragon recursion paradox".

def dragon_logic(depth=0):
    try:
        return "🦎" + dragon_logic(depth + 1)
    except RecursionError:
        return (
            "\n🐉 ERROR: the dragon bit its own tail.\n"
            "StackOverflow. Complexity: O(🐉), dragon scale.\n"
            "Want O(1)? wincent.com/careers"
        )

print(dragon_logic())

Task 1 · Website hero

The hero keeps the language of wincent.com: Nohemi Light headlines with one emphasised line, IBM Plex for text and labels, glass panels on a dark ground. The main call to action is flat Vivid Red, the Talent accent. Run in the sandbox is a quieter Dark Purple, so only one button leads the page. The new element is Byte, a mascot that reacts to code.

01

A hoard of one byte

Byte rests a claw on eight memory blocks: eight bits, one byte. They read 1101 1011, which is 0xDB in hex, the initials of DragonByte. The name of the challenge is shown literally.

02

The glow comes from inside

The brand manual describes the Talent stream as deep burgundy with an accent that erupts as pressure from within. Byte is built that way: dark carbon plates outside, Vivid Red light in the seams, magenta in the wings.

03

Run the code, wake the dragon

Pressing Run plays the program. Byte glows hotter as recursion depth climbs and flares at the recursion limit. The stack-depth chart crashes at the same moment, so the error message is shown before it is printed.

04

A joke only engineers get

The snippet is valid Python. It catches its own RecursionError, reports a complexity of O(🐉) and points to Wincent careers for anyone who wants O(1). It speaks to the audience in its own language.

The joke, explained

A classic programmer joke built on runaway recursion and a stack overflow. Software engineers, developers and computer science students get it at a glance. That is the technical talent Wincent wants to reach.

Recursion with no way out
dragon_logic() calls itself and adds one 🦎 per call. Nothing tells it to stop, so it only goes deeper.
The dragon bites its own tail
Python limits how deep calls can nest. The chart shows the stack climbing to 998 and crashing, and the interpreter raises RecursionError. The error message is the ouroboros, the mythical serpent that eats its own tail: a function consumed by itself.
O(🐉) versus O(1)
Big O notation describes how the cost of an algorithm grows. O(🐉) is deliberate nonsense for code that scales terribly. O(1) is constant time, the ideal.
The recruiting punchline
“Want O(1)? wincent.com/careers” turns the crash into a call to action: if you would rather write efficient code than crashing dragons, join the team.

Banner proposals · seven options

The same campaign as a banner, in the structure of the current DragonByte header: the Wincent DragonByte lockup, the mandatory name set in Press Start 2P and the prize line. The first two options carry the dragon, one in the navy and magenta of the Talent stream and one in gold; the other five try the contestant as the main figure, once with the layout mirrored. Each is 3000 × 1440 px, the format of the current header. The scenes are AI-generated images; the lockup and the type are set over them. The motion study is an AI-generated video; it starts muted and the sound can be turned on in its controls.

Task 2 · T-shirt

A tee for the people who take part, built so it outlives the edition. The back carries Byte, drawn straight out of the same 3D block-out as the hero render, under the line the concept already uses. The front carries one line of code and nothing else.

01

Worn after the event

No date, no edition number, no sponsor wall. Nothing on the garment ties it to one weekend, so it stays in the rotation. The only clue is 1101 1011 under the byte: 0xDB, for anyone who reads hex.

02

The same dragon, flattened for cotton

The back print is Byte projected straight out of the 3D block-out that the hero render was built from. Every plate is a polygon, so the artwork is vector at any size. The wings and the shaded sides turn into a coarse halftone instead of a gradient.

03

The line does the talking

The headline is the one from the concept: the dragon guards a byte, not gold. Around it sit the notes an engineer reads for free. Hoard = 1 byte (8 bits), 0xDB, 1101 1011, and the error the sandbox throws when the dragon bites its own tail.

04

Two screens, no gradients

White and Pantone 192 C on a navy body. Every tone is a real 45 degree halftone at about 8 lines per inch: coarse enough that the dot is visible on purpose, robust enough to hold on cotton through a hundred washes.

Three other directions for the artwork were drawn and dropped before this one. Every tile is the real artwork at the real print size, not a redraw of it, so the comparison is honest: the same two inks, the same 300 x 380 mm area, the same mascot underneath.

Print

Back: discharge screen print, two screens. Front: flat embroidery

Mesh
43T for the white, 48T for the red
Raster

Registration
Red spread 0.2 mm where it meets the white
Files
Vector PDF at 1:1, one per ink, type outlined

The front is embroidered because thirteen characters of mono type at 6 mm cap height stitch cleanly and give the shirt something to touch. The back is printed because 300 x 380 mm of embroidery would be heavy, stiff and expensive. Not DTG either: on a navy body it needs a white pre-treatment that leaves a visible panel, and above roughly forty shirts it costs more per piece. The discharge white is the reason the body has to be 100% cotton.

Earlier direction · Vintage Black

Where the tee started. Before the navy artwork above, the idea went through seven AI-generated tech-pack boards made with Gemini: a washed black heavyweight body, a puff-print or embroidered chest tag and a discharge print on the back. The boards are in Slovak. Their dimensions, size charts, approval stamps and small label texts are placeholders from the generator, not production data. The final tee above replaced this direction; the chest tag, the headline and the dragon carried over.

The Vintage Black tee worn: the tag at the chest, the print on the back, the fit. These three shots are AI-generated visualisations, not photographs of a produced sample. The back print in them is the framed artwork of this direction as the generator drew it, not the navy artwork above.

Task 3 · Business card

Quiet authority: one navy, one white logo, one column of type. Nothing decorative is printed on the contact side; the dragon is there only as gloss on matt, seen when the card catches the light. The back carries the typographic pattern, Navy Dark on Deep Navy. One 7.5 mm chamfer gives the card its orientation.

Both views are 3D visualisations, not photographs of a printed card.

Digital card · Swopi profile

The same contact as a digital card in Swopi: the profile, the contact sheet that saves to the phone in one tap, and the image settings with the typographic pattern as the cover and the dragon symbol as the logo. The screens come from a demo profile set up for this assignment.

NFC card · Swopi

The physical side of the digital card: a black NFC card from Swopi. The front carries the logo, the name and the title and nothing else; the back carries a QR code. A tap or a scan opens the profile above. The three views are previews from the Swopi card configurator, not photographs of a produced card.
